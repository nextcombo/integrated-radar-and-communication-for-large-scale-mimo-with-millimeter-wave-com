# Integrated Radar and Communication for Large-Scale MIMO with Millimeter Wave: Compression Sampling Perspective [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: Radio technology, wireless communication technology, and radar sensing technology have each achieved significant application results in the past few decades. Due to the increasing number of interconnected devices and applications leading to spectrum congestion, Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC) has attracted great research interest in recent years [1]-[6]. On one hand, ISAC allows communication systems and radar systems to share scarce spectrum and expensive hardware resources, saving a lot of costs. On the other hand, in some key scenarios beyond the fifth generation (B5G) or even beyond 6G, such as autonomous driving [7], Wi-Fi perception [8], and extended reality [9], there is a need for resilient communication and advanced radar technology to provide high-precision environmental perception capabilities. Therefore, it is expected that ISAC will benefit both the communication and radar communities in the near future.
In this paper, we focus on integrating radar systems with state-of-the-art wireless communication. Specifically, millimeter wave (mmWave) massive multiple input multiple output (mMIMO) systems are the backbone of physical layer technologies in 5G and beyond, and also an attractive solution for radar sensing. By leveraging the high angle resolution of mmWave mMIMO, the shape of targets can be clearly identified through the use of radar echo signals from different directions. Therefore, target imaging can be realized through radio frequency (RF) signals instead of simply detecting the presence of targets [10]. Thus, our goal is to integrate radar sensing into mmWave channel estimation (CE).
Despite ambitious visions, a key issue in the Assisted Inverse Situational Awareness (ISAC) system with MMIMO is the significant computational burden introduced by a large number of antennas in communication. Signal processing for communication and radar sensing is required. This problem becomes more pronounced when considering target imaging, as radar systems need to recover high-dimensional imaging information instead of estimating some parameters of the target. Moreover, low pilot overhead is a prerequisite for effective time-varying CE to ensure reliable service for users with high mobility in ISAC scenarios. Considering these challenges, Compressed Sensing (CS), also known as compressed sensing, can be a promising solution for ISAC systems by leveraging inherent sparsity from reduced measurements [11] to recover signals. Although CS technology has rapidly been promoted across many disciplines, further research on its application in ISAC systems is necessary.
Based on the above discussion, it is evident that mmWave mMIMO and CS technologies are still in an early development stage for their application in ISAC scenarios. Specifically, our main contributions can be summarized as follows.
·We propose a Directional Frequency Component Reconfigurable (DFRC) transceiver architecture based on mmWave mMIMO. The proposed architecture includes a communication unit (CU) and a radar unit (RU). The CU employs a critical interval array (CSA) with a HBF structure, while the RU uses a wide interval array (WSA) with a low resolution ADC to receive radar echo signals. The proposed architecture ensures energy-efficient communication and high angle resolution radar sensing, blurring the cost perspective.
· The study investigated the integration of radar perception with traditional cellular communication CE, proposing an ISAC frame structure and waveform design suitable for HBF architecture and CS processing. By considering different time scales, the proposed frame structure can be used to track rapidly changing environments with high mobility targets and serve users with high mobility. The proposed pilot waveform design not only fully diversifies the pilot waveforms as required by CS theory but also meets the hardware constraints imposed by the HBF architecture.
· We propose a dedicated algorithm based on CS to overcome the angle ambiguity brought about by WSA. By leveraging natural spatial consistency, specifically in situations where CU and RU observe the target from the same direction, we introduce an OMP-SR algorithm that supports fine-grained support estimation. The main idea is to eliminate the ambiguity of fine-grained angle estimation at each iteration, followed by refining the corresponding columns of the sensor matrix.
· A framework for Doppler frequency estimation is proposed to support target velocity measurement and effective payload data demodulation. During the transmission of payload data, a small number of pilot signals are inserted between adjacent data frames to estimate Doppler frequency (i.e., the speed of the target and user). In an ISAC system centered around communication, Doppler estimation and compensation are crucial for data demodulation, yet they were often overlooked in early ISAC research.

Conclusions: We have studied the ISAC system-aided mmWave mMIMO HBF architecture. Firstly, we introduce an energy-efficient WSA architecture as a radar receiver to improve the angle resolution of radar detection. Then, we design a time-varying ISAC system for the ISAC frame structure, which facilitates the estimation of angle, delay, and Doppler frequency. Specifically, the pilot waveform has been designed to meet the hardware constraints caused by the HBF array. To reduce pilot overhead, we formulate the ISAC processing formula into a sparse signal recovery problem with a dedicated dictionary, leveraging advanced compression-sensing techniques. Specifically, we propose the support vector refinement orthogonal matching pursuit algorithm, which can handle angle ambiguity and achieve better performance than traditional methods. We also provide a framework to estimate the Doppler frequency for users/targets, which is essential for velocity measurement and effective payload data demodulation. Future research directions include robust quantitative CS methods, interactions between radar perception algorithms and CE algorithms, beamforming design for transceiver architectures, near-field effects analysis, and conceptual validation experiments.
